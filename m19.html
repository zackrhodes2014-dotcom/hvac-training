1. Troubleshooting Electric Motors

Electrical or mechanical?

Motor problems are electrical (windings, capacitor, power, controls) or mechanical (bearings, blade, belt). Common symptoms:

  • Won't run, no sound: no power reaching it, an open overload, an open winding, or a control not calling.
  • Hums but won't start: a bad capacitor, a seized bearing, low voltage, or an open start winding.
  • Runs hot or trips the overload: low voltage, a weak capacitor, a dirty coil, or tight bearings.
  • Noisy: worn bearings, a loose blade, or a rubbing housing.

Winding faults (checked with power off and leads disconnected):

  • Open winding: infinite resistance (OL on the meter). The wire is broken.
  • Shorted winding: resistance lower than normal. Turns are touching each other.
  • Grounded winding: a path from a winding to the metal frame. Dangerous; replace the motor.

With power locked out, the shaft should spin freely by hand with no grinding or wobble.

2. Servicing Hermetic Compressor Motors

Reading C, S, and R

A single-phase compressor has two windings joined at Common. With power locked out and wires removed, Zach measures each terminal pair:

  • C to R (run winding) is the lowest.
  • C to S (start winding) is higher.
  • R to S is the highest, and it should equal C–R + C–S, since both windings are in series.
🧮 Worked exampleC–R = 0.8 Ω, C–S = 2.4 Ω.
  • R–S should be 0.8 + 2.4 = 3.2 Ω. If Zach reads 3.2 Ω, the windings check out.
  • If C–R and C–S read OL but R–S reads 3.2 Ω, the internal overload (in the common lead) is likely open. Let it cool, an hour or more, and recheck.

Ground test: each terminal to the shell or suction line should read open (infinite). A megohmmeter gives a better test.

Other checks: capacitor, start components, contactor, and voltage at the compressor during a start attempt (a live test, Zach only).

3. Servicing Fan Motors

Capacitors, bearings, and rotation

Most fan motors in residential equipment are PSC or ECM.

PSC fan motors

  • The run capacitor fails most often. It's rated in microfarads (µF) with a tolerance, often ±5% or ±6%. A bulging top or leaking oil means replace it.
  • After safely discharging it, Zach reads it in capacitance mode. Example: a 7.5 µF ±6% capacitor should read between 7.5 × 0.94 = 7.05 µF and 7.5 × 1.06 = 7.95 µF.
  • Replace with the same µF and an equal or higher voltage rating (370 V or 440 V).
  • Dual capacitors serve the compressor and fan in one can, with terminals marked C, HERM, and FAN.

ECM fan motors

  • Follow the manufacturer's procedure, often with a test tool, checking power and signal at the module. The module is sometimes replaceable alone.

Mechanical: check bearings, a loose or bent blade, rotation, and blade height in the shroud.

4. Servicing External Motors

Belts, bearings, and three-phase rotation

External (open) motors sit outside the equipment they drive, through a belt or coupling. Examples are belt-drive blowers, pumps, and open-drive compressors.

  • Belts: check tension (a common rule is 1/64 inch of deflection per inch of span), cracks, and glazing. Replace multi-belt sets together.
  • Alignment: a straightedge across both pulley faces checks it. Misalignment eats belts and bearings.
  • Bearings: oil or grease on schedule unless sealed; don't over-grease.
  • Couplings: check wear and alignment on direct-coupled pumps.
  • Three-phase rotation: swapping any two of the three power leads reverses the direction. Always verify rotation on startup.

5. Servicing Motor Control Systems

Contactors, relays, and overloads

  • Contactors: look for pitted, burned, or welded contacts, and ants jamming them. With power locked out, an open coil reading means bad. Match coil voltage, amp rating, and poles when replacing.
  • Start components: relays and PTC starters can stick. A stuck start relay leaves the start capacitor in circuit and destroys it.
  • Overloads: an open overload is usually a symptom. Find why the motor got hot.
  • Time delays and boards: confirm the delay is timing out, not failing.
  • Connections: tighten loose terminals and replace any discolored or melted wire ends. Heat damage spreads.

After any repair, run the unit and confirm voltage, amps, and operation match specs.
